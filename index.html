02 / THE CURIOSITY TRAIL

No straight line.
Just a lot of “what if?”

A journey told in chapters, not dates. These interests overlap—and many are still very much alive.

01

Curiosity switched on

Magnets, motors & a little magic

Homemade electromagnets, a brushed DC motor, an electric bell, mechanical drum sequencers and decorative lighting. I explored emergency lights, DC-to-AC conversion, a working gramophone model, tape recording, telescopes and microscopes.

02

Chasing invisible signals

The air was never empty.

SW, MW, LW and FM receivers, transmitters, antennas, Morse experiments and listening to amateur radio. Remote-controlled cars, early flying prototypes, small petrol engines and radio astronomy experiments turned invisible phenomena into something I could investigate.

03

Inside the machine

Opening things was the tutorial.

Computer hardware, printers, hard drives, memory and power supplies became a practical classroom. GW-BASIC and COBOL led to C/C++, assembly, low-level software and boot-level exploration. I also wrote small games and explored disk diagnostics.

04

Making useful things

From spare parts to side quests.

Computer repair, billing and inventory software, hardware and networking teaching, and running two affordable cyber cafés. Repurposed printer, floppy and optical-drive mechanisms became CNC drilling and antenna-positioning experiments. A battery-powered bicycle joined the list.

05

Code meets the real world

Now the ideas could move.

Atmel and PIC microcontrollers, robot prototypes, Linux and multi-system networking. I explored lathe work, forging, casting and milling, then combined fabrication, circuits and code in practical devices. EEG and brain–computer interface experiments opened another rabbit hole.

06

From bench to buildings

Small circuits. Bigger responsibilities.

RFID attendance, connected geysers, hydroponics control, soil testing equipment, hospital energy management, underground tracking and a room-addressable PA system spanning hundreds of rooms. I founded Ekworth Tech Pvt. Ltd. to build professionally.

07

Passing the spark on

Knowledge gets better when shared.

Thousands of student-project consultations across embedded systems, IoT, robotics, AI and ML. Dozens of workshops, STEM kits, physics teaching and hackathon mentoring—including teams that won first place. I keep learning through PLC/SCADA, FPGA and new experiments.

THE WORKBENCH METHOD

  1. Ask why.Understand the problem before choosing the board.
  2. Make something.A small working experiment beats a big assumption.
  3. Measure. Debug. Repeat.Let the behaviour tell you what needs to change.
  4. Pass it on.If you understand it, help someone else understand it.
